How hard is no?Measured monthly.

One hundred popular sites in four countries. Every month we check how many of them let you refuse on the first screen.

 

European rules say refusing should be as easy as accepting. This page shows how that works out on sites people actually visit, by name, with the method below so anyone can repeat it.

Method.

The crawler is open source: scripts/index in the BannerBye repository, with the fixed site list in sites.json. It runs from a Dutch connection, with the browser language set to the site's country. Sites that block automated browsers show up as errors and are left out of the percentages.

Every result is then checked by hand against the buttons the page actually showed. Where the hand check differs from the crawler, the hand check wins and the data file says so: banner-index.json.

A reject button counts when its label matches the same reject words the BannerBye extension uses, in the site's language. A pay-or-consent wall counts when there's a pay or subscribe button next to an accept button and no free way to refuse.

Questions

How does BannerBye measure the index?

Once a month we open the same fixed list of sites in a normal desktop browser, as a first-time visitor with no cookies and without BannerBye's own signals. We record whether the site asks for consent, whether a reject button is on the first screen, whether it's a pay-or-consent wall, and which consent platform it loads.

Then BannerBye's engine tries to refuse, and we record how many clicks that took.

Why these sites?

They are widely visited news, shopping and service sites in the Netherlands, Germany, France and Belgium, picked by us. It's a fixed list, not a ranking, so month-to-month changes mean the sites changed, not the sample.

Does the index use data from BannerBye users?

No. BannerBye has no telemetry. The index comes from our own browser, on our own machine.
